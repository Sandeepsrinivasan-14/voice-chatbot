12Experimental Evaluation

This section reports the empirical evaluation of the system’s central design hypothesis. The result was negative: the preprocessing layer, as originally implemented, substantially degraded recognition accuracy rather than improving it. The hypothesis, the measurement, the root-cause analysis, the corrective action, and the re-validation are reported in full.

12.1 Hypothesis

The design premise stated in §3.1 was that normalising loudness, pitch, and speaking rate before recognition would reduce the acoustic distance between modulated and typical speech, and would therefore improve recognition accuracy under whispered, shouted, slow, and fast delivery. The benchmark was constructed specifically so that this claim could be falsified rather than assumed.

12.2 Method

AspectDefinition
DesignWithin-subject, two-condition comparison. Every clip is transcribed twice — once unprocessed, once preprocessed — with all other parameters held constant.
Independent variableApplication of the preprocessing layer (absent / present).
Dependent variableWord recognition accuracy: the proportion of clips whose normalised transcription contains the expected word.
ControlledIdentical model (small), identical compute type, identical beam size (5 in both conditions), identical language setting, identical audio source.
ScoringCase-insensitive; punctuation stripped; the expected word must appear as a discrete token. This tolerates trailing punctuation and filler while rejecting substitutions.
Executionpython src/benchmark.py. Results written to benchmark_results.csv; chart rendered to accuracy_comparison.png.

12.3 Dataset

A purpose-recorded corpus of 25 clips: five command words — yes, no, stop, help, start — each spoken in five modulations: normal, whispered, shouted, slow, and fast. All clips were recorded by a single speaker through the browser recorder in a quiet domestic environment, at 16 kHz mono. The corpus is small, and §12.7 addresses the resulting limits on inference.

Preprocessing halved recognition accuracy. The degradation was not confined to the modulated conditions it was designed to assist — accuracy under normal delivery fell by 60 points, which is definitive evidence of a defect rather than a tuning deficiency. Representative failures included stop transcribed as “So…”, help as “L L L”, and one clip returning an empty transcription.

12.4.1 Ablation

Rather than adjusting parameters speculatively, the contribution of each stage was isolated by disabling stages individually and re-running the full corpus:

Table 12.2 — Controlled ablation of preprocessing stages
ConfigurationAccuracyInterpretation

Volume + pitch + speed (as shipped initially)48.0%Severe degradation.
Volume + pitch (speed correction disabled)92.0%Recovers most of the loss: speed correction is the dominant cause.
Volume only (pitch and speed disabled)96.0%Exact parity with baseline: pitch correction is a secondary contributor; amplitude normalisation is neutral.

12.4.2 Cause — speed correction

Speaking rate was estimated as onset density: detected onsets divided by total clip duration, compared against a nominal reference. This measure is only meaningful over continuous multi-syllable speech, where enough onsets accumulate to form a stable estimate. Applied to an isolated single command word it fails in two compounding ways. The word contributes approximately one onset, so the numerator is a sample of size one. The denominator is the entire recorded clip, most of which is silence surrounding the word — so the estimate is governed principally by how much padding the recording happens to contain, not by how quickly the speaker spoke.

The consequence was systematic misclassification. Every clip, in every modulation, was labelled “slow”. The clearest illustration is start_fast, genuinely spoken quickly, which was assigned a rate ratio of 0.22 — classified as extremely slow — and then time-stretched further in the wrong direction, producing audio degraded past intelligibility and an empty transcription. The design defect is precisely the violation of assumption ASM-03: a technique valid for continuous speech was applied to isolated words.

12.4.3 Cause — pitch correction

Pitch tracking on short, consonant-heavy words proved unreliable. On one start clip the tracker reported a median fundamental of 766 Hz — implausible for this speaker and consistent with locking onto a plosive or fricative burst rather than voiced phonation. The system then “corrected” a pitch anomaly that did not exist, damaging a word that required no intervention.

12.5 Corrective Action and Re-validation

Both correction stages were disabled by default through explicit feature flags. Detection and logging were retained, since the metadata remains diagnostically useful and carries no risk when it does not modify the signal. The benchmark was then re-executed unchanged:

12.7 Threats to Validity

ThreatTypeAssessment
Small corpus (n = 25)Statistical conclusionA single clip shifts overall accuracy by 4 percentage points and a per-modulation figure by 20. The 48-point degradation is far outside this margin and is therefore robust; the post-correction parity is correspondingly less precise, establishing “no material regression” rather than exact equivalence.
Single speakerExternalResults characterise this system with this voice. Accuracy and the appropriate confidence threshold may differ for other speakers, accents, and vocal ranges. This is also a fairness concern (§15.3).
Isolated words onlyExternalFindings do not transfer to continuous speech. Notably, the failure mode identified in §12.4.2 would not necessarily occur with multi-syllable utterances — the ablation justifies disabling the correction for this workload, not condemning the technique generally.
Quiet recording environmentExternalRobustness under background noise, reverberation, or competing speakers is untested.
Substring-based scoringConstructScoring rewards presence of the expected token, not exact transcription. Appropriate for command-word recognition; it would be inadequate for measuring full-sentence accuracy, where word error rate would be required.
Non-blinded executionInternalConditions were executed by the same automated script within one run, with the scoring function identical across conditions, which mitigates differential handling. No manual judgement entered the scoring.

12.8 Conclusions Drawn

  1. The recognition model is already substantially robust to vocal modulation on isolated command words: 96% accuracy with no preprocessing whatsoever, and perfect accuracy in four of five conditions.
  2. Amplitude normalisation is safe but, on this corpus, unnecessary — the model handled the loudness range unaided. It is retained because it is neutral and would plausibly matter under a wider dynamic range than this corpus exhibits.
  3. Pitch and rate correction, as implemented, were actively harmful for this workload and have been disabled with the evidence recorded and a regression test enforcing the decision.
  4. The residual robustness value of the system therefore lies in the confidence-gating layer, which addresses the failure mode identified in §3.1 directly and is not captured by this accuracy benchmark.
  5. Most significantly for engineering practice: the benchmark earned its cost by falsifying the project’s own design hypothesis. Had it not been built, a plausible but harmful component would have shipped with an unverified claim of improvement attached to it.

14Security Considerations

14.1 Trust Boundary

The system defines a single trust boundary: the host machine. Everything inside it — the browser, the application server, the model server, the synthesiser, and the filesystem — is treated as trusted. Nothing crosses the boundary. This is the system’s principal security property and its principal simplifying assumption: the design is sound precisely as long as that boundary holds, and degrades sharply if the service is exposed beyond it.

15Ethical Considerations

This section assesses the ethical dimensions of the system as designed and as evaluated. It addresses privacy, consent, fairness, transparency, accessibility, misuse, environmental cost, licensing obligations, and the integrity of reported results. Where the system falls short, that is stated rather than omitted.

15.1 Privacy and Data Sovereignty

Voice is among the most sensitive categories of personal data. It is biometric — a voiceprint can identify an individual — and it carries paralinguistic signals including emotional state, and in some cases health indicators. A conversational assistant is additionally exposed to whatever the speaker says in their own home, including content concerning third parties who never consented to processing.

The system’s response is architectural rather than procedural: no audio, transcript, or generated text leaves the machine. This is not a policy commitment that could be revised, nor a setting that could be misconfigured — there is no code path to any external endpoint. The practical consequences are substantial: no third-party processor agreement is required, no cross-border transfer arises, there is no vendor-side retention to negotiate, and no account or telemetry identifier exists to correlate. Under a data-protection framework such as the GDPR, processing remains entirely within the data subject’s own control, which materially reduces — though does not eliminate — the applicable obligations.

Design principle

Privacy here is a property of the architecture, not a feature layered on top of it. This is the strongest ethical claim the system can make, and it is also the reason the project deliberately declines to offer a hosted deployment (§2.3.2): doing so would forfeit the property that justifies the design.

15.2 Consent, Data Minimisation, and Retention

Two mechanisms capture voice. Conversational capture is explicitly initiated — the user presses a control to begin and to end recording; there is no wake word, no always-listening mode, and no ambient buffer retained beyond the ~300 ms pre-roll needed to avoid clipping speech onset. The browser additionally enforces its own permission prompt. Dataset recording is unambiguously deliberate: the evaluator is prompted per clip and reviews each recording before accepting it.

Data minimisation is largely satisfied: conversational audio is processed in memory and not persisted; conversation history exists only in browser memory and is discarded on reload. One exception is material and is stated plainly: the confidence audit log persists transcribed speech indefinitely, in plain text, with no rotation or purge. That log exists for a legitimate purpose — it is what makes the confidence threshold tunable from evidence and the gating behaviour auditable (§8.5.4) — but it constitutes a retention obligation that the system does not currently discharge.

RecommendationRationale
Introduce rotation and a retention limit on the confidence logAligns the audit trail with the minimisation applied elsewhere in the system.
Offer a redaction mode logging scores and decisions without transcribed textPreserves threshold-calibration utility while removing content.
Provide an explicit purge commandGives the data subject a practical means of erasure without manual file surgery.
Obtain informed consent from any additional speaker recorded into the corpusThe current corpus contains one speaker who is also the developer; this ceases to be self-evident with additional contributors.

15.3 Fairness and Bias

Two distinct bias surfaces exist, and both are documented rather than claimed absent.

15.3.1 Recognition bias

Speech recognition accuracy is well established to vary across accent, dialect, first language, age, and vocal characteristics correlated with gender. The recognition model is consumed as published; the project has not evaluated it across demographic groups and makes no claim about its equity. The reported 96% figure characterises one speaker.

15.3.2 Threshold calibration bias — a concrete, acknowledged defect

More specific to this system: the confidence threshold of −0.6 was calibrated against a single speaker’s recordings. Because the threshold governs whether a user is understood or asked to repeat, a speaker whose confidence scores are systematically lower — plausibly a speaker with a different accent, or a quieter or higher-pitched voice — would be asked to repeat more often for the same objective clarity of speech. A fixed threshold calibrated on one voice can therefore distribute the burden of the system’s uncertainty unequally.

MitigationStatus
Threshold is configurable per deploymentImplemented
Audit log provides the evidence needed to re-calibrateImplemented

Per-speaker adaptive calibrationNot implemented — §18

15.3.3 Language model bias

The language model reproduces biases present in its training data and may generate content that is stereotyped, culturally narrow, or factually wrong while remaining fluent. No fine-tuning, content filtering, or output moderation is applied beyond the stylistic system prompt. The observed arithmetic error during model comparison (§12.6) is a useful reminder that fluency and correctness are independent properties — a point that matters more, not less, when responses are delivered audibly and cannot be re-read.

15.4 Transparency and Explainability

The system makes its uncertainty visible rather than concealing it. The browser interface displays the transcription it acted upon together with its confidence score, and renders rejected transcriptions distinctly, showing the user both what was heard and why it was not trusted. The active compute device is surfaced. Every gating decision is recorded in an inspectable audit trail. This is a deliberate stance: a voice interface that silently substitutes words denies the user any means of understanding a failure, whereas one that says “I heard this, and I am not confident enough to act on it” keeps the user in control.

Limits of transparency are also acknowledged. avg_logprob is an uncalibrated proxy, not a probability of correctness; presenting it numerically to a non-technical user risks conveying more precision than it possesses. The language model itself is not explainable in any meaningful sense — the system can report what it heard, but not why the model answered as it did.

15.6 Misuse Potential

The system is a general-purpose local assistant with no capability to act on the world: it has no tool use, no file access on the user’s behalf, no network reach, and no persistent memory across sessions. Its misuse surface is correspondingly narrow, and the offline design removes the mass-surveillance concern that attends cloud voice assistants.

Two residual considerations are noted honestly. First, the same architecture that protects privacy also removes centralised safety oversight — an operator may substitute an unaligned model with no external control. This is an inherent property of local-first design, and the appropriate mitigation is operator responsibility rather than technical restriction. Second, the recorded voice corpus is voiceprint-grade biometric material; if such data were shared, it could contribute to voice cloning. The corpus is excluded from version control specifically to reduce the chance of inadvertent disclosure.

15.7 Environmental Impact

Inference draws power, and this is a genuine if modest cost. Several design decisions reduce it as a side effect of the VRAM constraint: quantised models require materially less computation than full-precision equivalents; a 3-billion-parameter model is used rather than a larger one; the recogniser loads once and is reused; and synthesis runs on CPU rather than occupying the GPU. Local inference also avoids the network transport and data-centre overhead of a hosted equivalent, though a consumer GPU is less energy-efficient per inference than optimised data-centre hardware, so the net comparison is not unambiguous. No lifecycle assessment was performed and none is claimed.

15.9 Integrity of Reported Results

A final ethical dimension concerns the reporting of this project’s own results. The preprocessing layer was the project’s stated central contribution. Measurement showed it reduced accuracy by 48 percentage points. That finding is reported here, in the README, and in the version-control history, with the full ablation and the corrective action — rather than being quietly removed, or the benchmark being re-scoped to a condition where the component appeared favourable.

This matters beyond the individual result. A benchmark constructed to confirm a hypothesis provides no information; a benchmark capable of refuting it is the only kind worth building. Reporting the refutation is what makes the remaining claims in this document credible.
